## Go beyond compliance

Natura11y is built to go beyond basic accessibility compliance by making accessible implementation simple and straightforward, no matter your experience level.

This page gives a bird's-eye view of Natura11y's built in accessibility features. For detailed guidance on accessibility best practices, see the <LinkOpenNew LinkUrl='https://www.w3.org/WAI/tips' LinkText='WAI guide' /> on accessibility basics.

</TextBlock>

</Section>

<Section id="semantic-html">

<TextBlock>

## Semantic HTML

Semantic HTML forms the foundation of web accessibility by using elements for their intended purpose. Natura11y components use proper semantic markup—headings, lists, buttons, and form controls—that assistive technologies can interpret and navigate.

Semantic markup improves keyboard accessibility, site performance, and SEO, and makes your code easier to maintain. Natura11y’s utility classes complement this semantic foundation, letting you style accessible HTML without changing its meaning.

</TextBlock>

</Section>

<Section id="color-contrast">

<TextBlock>

## Color and contrast

People have different needs when it comes to color. Natura11y includes coordinated themes and support for personal color preferences.

### Color themes

Each Natura11y theme pairs a background with colors for text, links, borders, and feedback messages. Apply a theme to a page or an individual component, and those colors work together within it.

When customizing a theme, check the contrast of your color combinations. The [Color documentation](/docs/color/) explains the available themes and related colors.

</TextBlock>

<TextBlock>

The examples above show a low-contrast color combination with a ratio of <em>1.91</em> beside one with stronger contrast at <em>5.84</em>.

</TextBlock>

<TextBlock>

### Light mode, dark mode, and prefers

Natura11y supports three options:

- **Light mode:** the default theme for a page or component.
- **Dark mode.** Use the dark theme for a page or component.
- **System preference (`prefers`).** Automatically switch the canvas theme to match the user’s system light or dark setting.

See [Theme color utilities](/docs/color/#theme-color-utilities) and [Prefers color scheme](/docs/color/#prefers-color-scheme) for setup and examples.

</TextBlock>

<TextBlock>

### Contrast themes

Contrast themes, such as those available in Windows, let people replace a site's colors with a palette that works for them. Natura11y responds automatically through the CSS `forced-colors` media query.

</TextBlock>

<FigureImage
    src="/docs-assets/accessibility/contrast-themes.jpg"
    alt="Website with yellow navigation links, white text, and outlined controls on a black background."
/>

<TextBlock>

Support includes:

- **Component borders.** Buttons, alerts, accordions, menus, and overlay panels keep defined edges, including search buttons inside fields.
- **Tab selection.** Active bars, dividers, and selected-tab styles keep the current tab identifiable.
- **Arrows and switches.** Arrows retain their shape; switches keep a visible track and position indicator.
- **Keyboard focus.** Outlines remain dedicated to focus and stay separate from component borders.

Learn more in <LinkOpenNew LinkUrl="https://accessibility.huit.harvard.edu/news/2026/05/deep-dive-contrast-themes" LinkText="Harvard's guide to contrast themes" />.

</TextBlock>

<Section id="aria">

<TextBlock>

## ARIA

Accessible Rich Internet Applications (<LinkOpenNew LinkUrl='https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/ARIA' LinkText='WAI-ARIA' />) roles, states, and properties appear throughout Natura11y's components. Some are added with JavaScript; others are included in the example markup. Add ARIA attributes when building custom interactions or when semantic HTML does not fully convey a component's purpose to assistive technologies.

</TextBlock>

<FigureExample
	figureNumber="1"
	figureWidth="narrow"
	showCodeFrame={false}
	code={(`
	<!-- Aria Role -->
	<div class="alert" role="alert">
			<!-- alert content -->
	</div>

	<!-- Aria State -->
	<button aria-expanded="false">
			Accordion Button
	</button>

	<!-- Aria Property -->
	<div
			class="modal__content"
			aria-labelledby="modal-title">
			<!-- modal content -->
	</div>
	`)}

<Section id="focus-indication">

<TextBlock>

## Keyboard and focus

Natura11y ensures focus indicators remain visible and correctly positioned. Focus rings adapt to theme colors—so they don't vanish on matching backgrounds. They also avoid clipping from parent containers using `overflow: hidden`, a common issue in other frameworks.

</TextBlock>

<FigureImage
	src="/docs-assets/accessibility/focus-indication.jpg"
	alt="Buttons showing focus styles in light, primary, secondary, and dark themes."
	caption="Depiction of focus styles across themes."
/>

<TextBlock>

Modal dialogs keep keyboard focus inside while they are open and close with <kbd>Escape</kbd>. When you close a modal, flyout, or lightbox, focus returns to the triggering element.

Use <kbd>Tab</kbd> and <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Tab</kbd> to move between controls. Components such as tabs and menus support <kbd>Arrow</kbd> keys for navigation within the component.

Skip links let keyboard users move past repeated navigation and reach the main content. Natura11y includes styles that reveal these links when they receive focus.

</TextBlock>

</Section>

<Section id="pointer-touch-target-sizes">

<TextBlock>

## Touch target sizes

A target is the area of a link, button, or form control that responds to a click or tap. Larger targets with enough space between them are easier to activate, especially for people with limited dexterity or anyone using a touchscreen.

Natura11y’s buttons and form fields use shared minimum heights and padding that scale with text size. When customizing controls, check their full clickable or tappable area and the space between neighboring targets. This includes icon-only buttons, whose target can be larger than the visible icon.

See <LinkOpenNew LinkUrl="https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html" LinkText="WAI’s target-size guidance" /> for size and spacing requirements and their exceptions.

</TextBlock>

</Section>

<Section id="reduced-motion">

<TextBlock>

## Prefers reduced motion

Motion and animation can cause discomfort or disorientation for people with vestibular disorders, epilepsy, or other motion sensitivities. The `prefers-reduced-motion` media query lets users signal through their operating system that they prefer less motion.

Every Natura11y component that includes animation or transitions—accordions, alerts, carousels, collapse panels, flyouts, and form toggles—automatically disables those effects when this preference is active. No extra configuration required.

<LinkOpenNew LinkUrl='https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C39' LinkText='Learn more about prefers-reduced-motion' />

</TextBlock>

</Section>

<Section id="built-in-accessible-components">

<TextBlock>

## Built-in accessible components

Most frameworks leave you searching for accessible third-party plugins that break your design system's consistency. Natura11y includes a complete library of accessible components out of the box—from complex interactions like modals and carousels to essential form controls and navigation patterns.

Components use WCAG-aligned patterns and integrate with Natura11y's theme system, giving teams a strong accessibility foundation. Final conformance still depends on the content, composition, and testing of the product that uses them.

<ul class="grid gap-2 margin-y-4">

<FeatureCard
  title='Carousel'
>

The [Track component](/docs/track) is an accessible carousel with <kbd>Arrow</kbd> key navigation, ARIA live region announcements, and <kbd>Tab</kbd> focus limited to visible panels only.

</FeatureCard>

<FeatureCard
  title='Flyout'
>

The [Flyout](/docs/flyout) uses `role="dialog"` and `aria-modal`, traps focus inside when open, returns focus on close, and supports drill-down panel navigation with `inert` to hide inactive panels from assistive technology.

</FeatureCard>

<FeatureCard
  title='Forms'
>

Complete [form](/docs/form) system with proper labeling, semantic fieldsets, accessible file uploads, and error handling.

</FeatureCard>

<FeatureCard
  title='Lightbox'
>

[Lightbox](/docs/lightbox) includes focus trapping, keyboard controls, and seamless gallery support for accessible image and video viewing.

</FeatureCard>

<FeatureCard
  title='Main menu'
>

The [Main menu](/docs/main-menu) collapses for mobile with `aria-expanded` and expands to full keyboard-navigable dropdowns and mega menus at desktop — one accessible component across both viewports.

</FeatureCard>

</ul>

</TextBlock>
